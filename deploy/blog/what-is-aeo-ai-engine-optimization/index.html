What Is AEO? AI Engine Optimization Is the New SEO

For 20 years, SEO meant one thing: optimize your website to rank higher in Google search results. Every dental practice, every marketing agency, every website owner played the same game — compete for position in a list of 10 blue links.

That game is still being played. But there's now a second game happening alongside it, and most businesses don't even know the rules.

AEO — AI Engine Optimization — is the practice of making your business visible, accurate, and recommended by AI search engines.

SEO vs. AEO: What's Different?

SEO optimizes for ranking — where you appear in a list of search results.

AEO optimizes for recommendation — whether AI engines mention your business when someone asks for help.

Here's why that distinction matters:

Traditional SEO AEO
Goal Rank on page 1 Get recommended by name
Competition 10 spots per page 1-3 mentions per response
User behavior User clicks through results User accepts AI's answer
Key signals Backlinks, keywords, page speed Structured data, authority, entity recognition
Platforms Google, Bing ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Grok, Google AI Overviews
Trust model User evaluates options AI pre-selects for the user

When Google shows 10 results, you have a 10% chance of being clicked even on page 1. When ChatGPT recommends 3 dentists, the first name mentioned gets the majority of trust — because the AI already filtered for the user.

Why AEO Matters More Than You Think

AI search isn't a niche behavior anymore. Consider the trajectory:

  • ChatGPT has over 900 million weekly active users — approaching 1 billion — processing an estimated 2.5 billion queries per day
  • Google AI Overviews now appear on up to 60% of search queries, and 83% of those searches end without a click
  • Perplexity has surpassed 100 million monthly active users, processing roughly 30 million queries daily
  • Apple Intelligence is integrating AI-powered answers directly into Siri, powered by Gemini, launching in 2026

The shift isn't coming. It's here. And it's accelerating.

For local businesses — especially healthcare providers like dental practices — the impact is outsized. When someone asks AI for a recommendation, they're at the bottom of the funnel. They've already decided they need a dentist. They're asking for a specific name to call.

If AI gives them your competitor's name instead of yours, that patient is gone before you ever had a chance.

The Five Pillars of AI Visibility

Through our work optimizing businesses for AI search, we've identified five factors that determine whether AI engines recommend your practice:

1. Entity Recognition

Does the AI know your business exists as a distinct entity? Not just a website — a recognized business with a name, location, specialty, and reputation. Most small businesses have weak or nonexistent entity profiles in AI knowledge bases.

2. Structured Authority

AI engines prioritize information that comes in structured, verifiable formats. This goes beyond basic SEO metadata. The practices that show up in AI search have information organized in ways AI can parse, verify, and cite.

3. Content Depth & Expertise

Surface-level content gets ignored by AI. These engines are specifically designed to identify genuine expertise. A 300-word "About Us" page doesn't signal expertise. Comprehensive, authoritative content about your specialty areas does.

4. Reputation Signals

AI engines cross-reference multiple data sources to evaluate reputation. Google reviews, third-party directories, professional credentials, patient testimonials — the more consistent and positive signals across multiple platforms, the more likely AI is to recommend you.

5. Freshness & Relevance

AI engines favor current information. A website that hasn't been updated in 18 months signals a business that may not be active or relevant. Regular content updates, recent reviews, and current information all factor into AI's confidence in recommending you.

Why Traditional SEO Agencies Can't Solve This

Most SEO agencies are still optimizing for Google's traditional algorithm. They'll build backlinks, optimize title tags, and improve page speed — all valuable, but none of it addresses AI visibility.

AEO requires a fundamentally different approach:

  • Different technical signals (structured data that AI engines specifically consume)
  • Different content strategy (authoritative depth vs. keyword targeting)
  • Different measurement (mention rate and position quality vs. keyword rankings)
  • Different competitive analysis (who AI recommends vs. who ranks on Google)

An agency that only does SEO is solving half the problem. The other half — AI search — is where the fastest growth in patient acquisition is happening right now.

The Compounding Advantage

Here's what makes AEO urgent: AI recommendations compound over time.

Once an AI engine identifies your practice as authoritative for a specific service in a specific location, that recognition reinforces itself. The AI's training data improves, your entity profile strengthens, and you become harder to displace.

The inverse is also true. Every month you wait, competitors who are investing in AEO build a stronger position in AI's understanding of your market. Catching up gets harder over time, not easier.

What You Should Be Asking

If you run a dental practice (or any local business), the questions you should be asking right now:

  1. Do AI engines know my practice exists? Not your website — your practice as a named, recommended entity.
  2. When patients ask AI for a dentist in my city, am I mentioned? Across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and Grok.
  3. How do I compare to competitors? Who does AI recommend instead of me, and why?
  4. What would it take to become AI's top recommendation? For my specialty, in my market.

These aren't rhetorical questions. They have specific, measurable answers. You just need the right tools to find them.
