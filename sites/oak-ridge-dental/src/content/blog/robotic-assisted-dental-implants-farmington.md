If you're missing a tooth — or several — you already know it affects more than your smile. It changes how you chew, how you speak, and over time it can even shift neighboring teeth and shrink the jawbone underneath the gap. Dental implants are the closest thing modern dentistry has to a brand-new natural tooth, and at **Oakridge Dental in Farmington**, we place them using **robotic-assisted (Yomi) technology** for a level of precision that wasn't possible a few years ago.

This guide walks through exactly what that means for you: how the technology works, why precision matters, who's a good candidate, what the process and recovery look like, and how to think about cost.

## What is a dental implant, exactly?

A [dental implant](/services/dental-implants) is a small, biocompatible titanium post that acts as an artificial tooth *root*. It's placed into the jawbone, where it fuses with the bone over a few months in a process called **osseointegration**. Once it's stable, we attach a custom crown, bridge, or denture on top.

Because the implant replaces the root — not just the visible tooth — it does something no other option can: it keeps the jawbone stimulated and healthy, preventing the "sunken" look that often follows tooth loss.

A complete implant has three parts:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Implant post** | The titanium root that anchors into the jawbone |
| **Abutment** | The connector that links the post to the restoration |
| **Restoration** | The visible [crown](/services/dental-crowns-bridges), bridge, or denture |

## What makes robotic-assisted implants different?

Traditional implant placement relies entirely on the dentist's training, experience, and steady hand working "freehand." It works — but every millimeter matters when you're placing a post near nerves and sinuses.

Robotic guidance adds GPS-like accuracy on top of that expertise. The **[Yomi system](https://www.neocis.com/)** is the first and only robotic system cleared by the U.S. FDA for dental surgery. Here's how it works:

1. **3D planning.** We take a cone-beam CT (CBCT) scan that shows your bone, nerves, sinuses, and existing teeth in three dimensions. We then plan the ideal position, angle, and depth for each implant on a computer — before any procedure begins.
2. **Real-time haptic guidance.** During surgery, Yomi physically guides the handpiece. If the plan says the drill should stop at a certain depth or stay at a precise angle, the system provides physical feedback that keeps everything on track.
3. **Live navigation.** The dentist watches an on-screen view that updates in real time, like a navigation system for the procedure — while remaining in complete control of every movement.

The key point: **the technology doesn't replace the dentist — it makes an experienced dentist even more precise.**

## Why that precision actually matters to you

Greater accuracy isn't just a technical detail. It translates into real benefits:

- **Better long-term results.** Implants placed in the ideal position look more natural and function more comfortably for decades.
- **Protecting what's nearby.** Precise planning helps the dentist avoid sensitive structures like the inferior alveolar nerve and the sinus cavities — the structures responsible for most implant complications.
- **Often less invasive.** Guided placement can mean smaller incisions and, in many cases, a more comfortable recovery.
- **More predictable.** When the surgery follows a digital plan exactly, there are fewer surprises and a smoother path to your final restoration.

## Who is a candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults missing one or more teeth are candidates. The best way to know for sure is a consultation and 3D scan, where we evaluate:

- **Bone volume.** Implants need enough healthy bone to anchor into. If you've been missing a tooth for a while and the bone has shrunk, a [bone graft](/services/bone-grafting) can rebuild the foundation first.
- **Gum health.** Active gum disease needs to be treated before implant placement, since healthy gums support long-term success.
- **Overall health.** Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking can affect healing — we'll talk through your health history together.

Implants can replace:

- **A single tooth** — without grinding down healthy neighbors the way a traditional bridge requires.
- **Several teeth** — with an implant-supported bridge.
- **A full arch** — with [All-on-4 implants](/services/all-on-4-implants), which anchor a full set of fixed teeth on just four strategically placed implants.

## The implant process, step by step

Every case is a little different, but here's the typical journey:

1. **Consultation & 3D scan.** We review your goals, take a CBCT scan, and build your custom plan. You'll get a clear written estimate before anything begins.
2. **Foundation work (if needed).** If you need a [bone graft](/services/bone-grafting) or a tooth [extraction](/services/tooth-extractions) first, that happens now, followed by a short healing period.
3. **Implant placement.** Using Yomi guidance, we place the implant — usually 30 to 60 minutes per implant, with the area fully numbed and [sedation](/services/iv-sedation) available if you'd like it.
4. **Healing (osseointegration).** Over about 3 to 6 months, the implant fuses with your bone. Many patients wear a temporary tooth during this time so they're never without a smile.
5. **The final restoration.** Once the implant is fully integrated, we attach your custom crown, bridge, or denture — color-matched to blend in seamlessly.

## Recovery and aftercare

Implant recovery is usually milder than people expect. For the first few days:

- Some swelling and mild soreness are normal and respond well to over-the-counter pain relievers.
- Stick to softer foods and avoid the surgical site while it heals.
- Keep the area clean as directed — gentle rinsing helps.

Once healed, an implant is cared for exactly like a natural tooth: brush, floss, and keep up with regular cleanings and exams. That routine care is what helps implants last for decades.

## Dental implants vs. the alternatives

How do implants compare to the other ways to replace missing teeth?

| Option | Replaces the root? | Protects jawbone? | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dental implant** | Yes | Yes | Decades to lifetime |
| **Fixed bridge** | No | No | 10–15 years |
| **[Dentures](/services/dentures)** | No | No | 5–10 years (relines needed) |

Bridges and dentures are excellent solutions in the right situations, and we offer all of them. But when it comes to preserving bone and feeling closest to a natural tooth, implants stand apart.
