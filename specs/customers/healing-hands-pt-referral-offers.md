# Healing Hands Physical Therapy & Bodywork — Referral Program + Growth Offers

**Customer:** Healing Hands PT & Bodywork — Reno, NV. Dr. Jamie Pribyl, PT/DPT/MTC.
**Model:** Cash-pay, out-of-network, hands-on concierge PT. No insurance billing.
**Services:** Concierge PT, Manual Therapy, Dry Needling, StemWave (acoustic shockwave), CranioSacral, Myofascial Release, Cupping, Muscle Energy, Visceral Mobilization. Sells **session packages** + **monthly maintenance memberships**.
**Business problem:** Too much customer turnover. Needs to GROW (new patients via referral) AND RETAIN (keep patients on maintenance).
**Seed idea (Kody):** "For every referral who signs up for a 10-session package minimum, the referrer gets 1 free session."

> **PRICING IS A GATE.** This spec does NOT fabricate Jamie's prices. Every economic estimate below is reasoned relative to her unknown session value / package price / membership price. Anything marked **[NEEDS JAMIE'S NUMBERS]** must be filled before launch.

## 1. RESEARCH — How referral programs that actually work are built

### 1a. Double-sided ("give-get") beats single-sided — and it's the dominant pattern

The highest-performing referral programs reward **both** the referrer and the new customer, not just the referrer.

- **Two-sided rewards work ~2× better than single-sided rewards** across multiple independent studies. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- **Dropbox**: both sides got 500 MB free per successful referral → **3,900% growth in 15 months** (100k → 4M users); ~60% signup increase; 2.8M referrals in 18 months. ([Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/), [Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- **PayPal**: two-sided cash (capped at 10 referrals / up to $100), drove 7–10% *daily* growth to 100M+ members. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- The reason it converts better: **both sides have something at stake**, and the framing reads as a gift/favor rather than a commission/affiliate play (which patients dislike from a healthcare provider). ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))

**Important cost nuance:** double-sided does NOT mean double cost. Splitting one reward into two halves keeps total cost flat. A $20 advocate reward → $10 advocate + $10 friend is still $20. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))

### 1b. Referred customers are *better* customers (this is why referral beats paid ads for retention)

- Referred customers have **~20% higher retention** than paid-ad-acquired customers; referrals carry pre-established trust → lower churn. ([growth-onomics](https://growth-onomics.com/retention-vs-churn-clv-impact/))
- Referrals delivered **2.8× more signups per dollar** than Google AdWords (Dropbox era). ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- Retention math that justifies "spending" a free session to win a referral: a **5% increase in retention can lift profit 25–95%**, and selling to an existing customer is **up to 14× more likely** than to a new one (Wharton / Prof. David Reibstein). ([Wharton Exec Ed](https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/thought-leadership/wharton-online-insights/why-customer-lifetime-value-matters/), [growth-onomics](https://growth-onomics.com/retention-vs-churn-clv-impact/))

**Takeaway for Healing Hands:** a referred patient who buys a 10-pack is likely to *stay on maintenance longer* than a cold lead. The free session given to the referrer is cheap relative to the LTV of a retained, referred patient.

### 1c. The ask: timing and script (the "success moment")

- **Ask at the success moment** — right after a transformation/win, while the patient is "basking in the positive outcome." For PT that's the visit where pain noticeably drops or mobility returns. ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/customer-referral-program-ideas/), Hormozi via [learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/))
- **Hormozi's script: "Who else do you know who…"** — forces the patient to surface specific names instead of a yes/no. "Who else can we help feel this good?" outperforms "do you know anyone?" ([learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/), [DeepCast Ep 729](https://deepcast.fm/episode/7-obscenely-easy-ways-to-get-new-customers-this-week-ep-729))
- **Make the referral a condition of a discount/perk:** when you give a patient something (a discount, an upgrade), ask for 3 names in return — turning a giveaway into 3 warm leads. ([learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/))
- **Repeat the "bring a friend" message everywhere** (emails, texts, in-room signage). Frame it as: you'll get better results *together* — more accountability, more support. ([learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/))
- Place the referral ask on **physical handouts, post-visit texts/emails, and the booking confirmation** — the moment "the excitement of buying is fresh." ([Yelp for Business](https://business.yelp.com/resources/articles/referral-program-ideas/), [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/customer-referral-program-ideas/))

### 1d. Mechanics that work for *recurring* service businesses (the Healing Hands shape)

From service-business referral best practice ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/customer-referral-program-ideas/), [LocalIQ](https://localiq.com/blog/customer-referral-program/)):

- **Recurring providers win with service credits / free service**, not external gift cards — keeps value inside the relationship and reinforces the maintenance habit. (Jamie's free-session reward fits this perfectly.)
- **Unique referral links / codes** so credit is automatic and tracked. Jobber reports a **~70% win rate** on tracked referrals.
- **Gate the reward on a qualified, paid conversion** (not a signup) — reward fires only when the referred person *buys*. This is the single most important guardrail against margin leakage and gaming. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- **Caps + time windows** (Tesla capped at 5/year; PayPal at 10): redeem-within-X-days closes open liability and prevents abuse. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- **Win-back / reactivation** of lapsed patients costs less than new acquisition and is a standard recurring-revenue lever. ([GymMaster](https://www.gymmaster.com/blog/seasonal-gym-marketing-ideas-to-boost-memberships/))

### 1e. Hormozi framing applied

Hormozi's referral logic: **referrals are the highest-leverage lead source** because customers acquire customers for you. They flow from **goodwill created by overdelivering** — deliver more than expected, get the win faster, make it easy → patients become advocates. Then **make the double-sided ask explicit** (model after PayPal/Dropbox: both sides win) at the success moment, and **bake "bring a friend" into every touchpoint.** ([learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/), [DeepCast Ep 729](https://deepcast.fm/episode/7-obscenely-easy-ways-to-get-new-customers-this-week-ep-729))

For a concierge cash-pay PT, "overdeliver" is structural: she already does long hands-on 1:1 sessions. The referral program is the *capture mechanism* on top of goodwill she's already generating.

## 2. COMPLIANCE FLAG — *Not legal advice. Verify before launch.*

**Bottom line: a pure cash-pay / out-of-network referral reward is materially lower-risk than the same offer in an insurance practice — but it is NOT zero-risk, mostly because of NEVADA's PT-board "unearned fee" rule and Google/FTC review rules.**

### 2a. Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) & Stark — largely NOT triggered here

- AKS prohibits remuneration to induce/reward referrals **for items or services payable by a Federal health care program** (Medicare/Medicaid). Stark restricts physician self-referral of **designated health services payable by Medicare/Medicaid**. ([HHS OIG](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/physician-education/fraud-abuse-laws/), [ASA](https://www.asahq.org/quality-and-practice-management/managing-your-practice/timely-topics-in-payment-and-practice-management/anti-kickback-statute-and-physician-self-referral-laws-stark-laws))
- **The federal hook is the federal payer.** With **no insurance billing and no federally-reimbursed service**, the core AKS/Stark/beneficiary-inducement machinery generally does not attach to a patient-to-patient word-of-mouth reward. This is why cash-pay is "materially looser."
- **HARD GUARDRAIL:** Do **NOT** extend any referral incentive to a service or patient that touches a federal payer. If Jamie ever bills (or a referred patient is a Medicare/Medicaid beneficiary receiving a federally-reimbursed service), the analysis changes. Keep the program **strictly cash-pay only**, in writing.

### 2b. "We don't take insurance" ≠ zero risk

Even pure cash-pay healthcare businesses can face **state anti-kickback, fee-splitting, and inducement** exposure — the residual risk is **state-law and professional-board**, not federal AKS. ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/but-we-dont-take-insurance-why-cash-pay-digital-health-companies-still-face-kickback-and-fee-splitting-risk.html)) The biggest one for Jamie is her own licensing board:

### 2c. Nevada PT Board — the "unearned fee" rule (the real one to check)

Nevada law (NRS/NAC 640) defines an **"unearned fee"** as a *"credit, gratuity, commission, professional discount or wage in consideration for the referral of a patient by that person to the physical therapist,"* and entering into such an arrangement is **grounds for disciplinary action.** ([NAC 640 / Justia §640.670](https://regulations.justia.com/states/nevada/chapter-640/disciplinary-action/section-640-670/), [NRS 640.160](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-640/statute-640-160/))

- **Why this likely targets professional fee-splitting, not patient word-of-mouth:** the rule is aimed at paying a *referral source* (e.g., another provider, a marketer, anyone "in consideration for the referral of a patient"). A satisfied **patient** rewarding a friend is a different fact pattern than paying a steering source for a stream of patients.
- **BUT** the statutory language is broad ("any person," "gratuity"), and a free session given for sending a patient could plausibly be read into it. **This is the #1 item for Jamie to clear with a NV healthcare attorney and/or a quick informal read from the NV Physical Therapy Board before launch.**
- **Risk-reducing program design (recommend regardless):**
  - Frame rewards as **service credit toward the referrer's own care** (keeps value inside her clinical relationship), not cash or transferable gift cards.
  - Cap rewards and don't make it a standing **commission per head** (which reads most like an "unearned fee").
  - Position the program as a **patient appreciation / thank-you**, double-sided, available to all patients — not a paid bounty.

### 2d. Google reviews / FTC — do NOT pay for reviews

- **Google prohibits any incentive (discount, cash, free service, gift card, loyalty points) in exchange for a review.** Violations → review removal, warning banner, or listing suspension. ([WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/blog/google-review-incentives/), [Applause](https://www.applausehq.com/blog/googles-rules-for-incentivizing-reviews))
- **FTC 2024 rule:** up to **$51,744 per violation** for deceptive/incentivized review practices; **review gating** (soliciting only positive reviews) is prohibited. ([RaveCapture](https://ravecapture.com/resources/blog/review-incentives-whats-allowed-and-what-actually-works/), [WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/blog/google-review-incentives/))
- **Compliant pattern:** never offer a reward *for* a review. You may *ask* for an honest review and *thank* people who leave one — with no pre-promised reward. The "review offer" (Offer #6) is therefore reframed below to stay legal.

### 2e. Compliance summary

| Rule | Applies to Jamie? | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Federal AKS / Stark / beneficiary inducement | **Largely NO** (no federal payer) — keep it that way | Written rule: cash-pay only; never touch a Medicare/Medicaid service |
| State anti-kickback / fee-splitting | Low but nonzero | Use service-credit (not cash bounty) framing |
| **NV PT Board "unearned fee" (NAC/NRS 640)** | **Maybe — must clear** | **Attorney/board check before launch; design as patient appreciation, capped, credit-based** |
| Google + FTC review rules | **YES** | Never reward reviews; ask + thank only |

**One-line bottom line:** Cash-pay status removes the federal anti-kickback teeth, so the program is *probably fine* — but get a 30-minute attorney/PT-board read on the Nevada "unearned fee" rule, structure rewards as in-clinic service credit (not cash), and never tie any reward to a Google review.

## 3. THE OFFERS — 6 testable plays, ranked by expected impact

**Economics convention:** her marginal cost of a "free session" is mostly her *time*, not cash (cash-pay, hands-on). So the real cost of a free session ≈ the **opportunity cost of one booked slot**, which is far below the session's list price. That's what makes free-session rewards cheap relative to the LTV of a retained, referred patient. **[NEEDS JAMIE'S NUMBERS: session list price, 10-pack price, membership price, avg patient lifetime in months]** to compute exact CAC and reward-cost ratios.

### Ranked

| Rank | Offer | Primary metric | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Double-sided "you both get a session"** | Acquisition (+ retention) | 2× lift of single-sided; lowest-friction; new patient gets a risk-free try |
| **2** | **Membership referral → free month of maintenance** | **Retention** (the stated #1 problem) | Directly attacks turnover; deepens the maintenance habit on both sides |
| **3** | **Seed: free session per referral who buys a 10-pack** | Acquisition | Strong qualified gate; her own idea; great as the single-sided control arm |
| **4** | **Reactivation / win-back** | Reactivation | Cheapest patients to win are lapsed ones; low effort, recurring |
| **5** | **Family / partner package** ("treat together") | Acquisition + retention | Built-in accountability ("get better together"); raises basket size |
| **6** | **"Bring a friend" first-visit + review *ask*** | Acquisition (+ reputation) | Lowest barrier entry point; review piece must be ask-only (compliant) |

---

### Offer #1 — Double-sided "You both get a free session" ⭐ TEST FIRST
- **Mechanic:** Existing patient refers a friend. When the friend **books and completes their first paid visit (or buys any package)**, *both* the referrer and the new patient get **1 free session** (or session credit).
- **Who gets what:** Referrer = 1 free session. New patient = 1 free session (applied to their care).
- **Gate:** Reward fires only on the friend's **first paid conversion** — never on a booking/no-show. ([Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/how-to-launch-a-double-sided-referral-program))
- **Metric:** Acquisition primarily; retention secondary (both sides get pulled deeper in).
- **Economics:** Two free sessions = two booked-slot opportunity costs. If a new patient who completes a first visit converts to a package/membership at a reasonable rate, the LTV dwarfs 2 slots. **[NEEDS NUMBERS to confirm reward-cost < ~15% of referred LTV.]**
- **A/B test:** Arm A = double-sided (both get a session). Arm B = single-sided seed (Offer #3, only referrer gets it on a 10-pack). Primary KPI: **referred-patient conversions per 100 active patients per month.** Run 6–8 weeks. Expect Arm A to win on *volume of referrals started*; watch whether Arm B wins on *quality* (10-pack gate filters for committed patients).

### Offer #2 — Membership referral → free month of maintenance ⭐ TEST FIRST (retention play)
- **Mechanic:** A patient **on monthly maintenance** refers a friend who **enrolls in maintenance membership** → referrer gets **1 free month**; new member gets a **discounted or waived first month** (or a free add-on like a cupping/needling session).
- **Who gets what:** Referrer = 1 free maintenance month. New member = first-month perk.
- **Gate:** New member must **enroll and complete first billing cycle** (not just sign up) before the free month posts.
- **Metric:** **Retention** (the stated core problem) + acquisition of recurring-revenue patients. A free month *extends* the referrer's own membership lifetime, directly fighting turnover.
- **Economics:** "Free month" cost = the sessions in one maintenance month (her time), offset against (a) the referrer staying enrolled longer and (b) a *new recurring* member. Recurring revenue is the highest-LTV outcome. The Wharton retention math (5% retention → 25–95% profit) is the justification. **[NEEDS NUMBERS: membership price + sessions/month included.]**
- **A/B test:** Offer free-month vs. a 50%-off-month variant; measure **net new active memberships** and **referrer membership-renewal rate** at 90 days. Free month should win on advocacy; 50%-off may win on margin — pick by which moves *net membership months*.

### Offer #3 — Seed: 1 free session per referral who buys a 10-pack (single-sided control)
- **Mechanic (Kody's seed, kept as a tracked arm):** Referrer gets **1 free session** for every referred patient who **buys a 10-session package (minimum).**
- **Who gets what:** Referrer only (single-sided). Optionally add a small new-patient perk to soften it.
- **Gate:** Hard, high-quality gate = **referred patient purchases a ≥10-pack.** Filters for committed, high-LTV patients.
- **Metric:** Acquisition of high-intent, high-value patients.
- **Economics:** Best reward-to-value ratio of all offers (1 slot given vs. a 10-pack sold). The risk is **lower referral volume** because the gate is steep and the friend gets nothing → less word-of-mouth pull. That's exactly why it's the *control* against the double-sided arms.
- **A/B test:** This is the control arm for Offers #1 and #2. KPI: **referrals-started rate** and **referred-patient package-purchase rate.**

### Offer #4 — Reactivation / win-back
- **Mechanic:** Lapsed patients (no visit in N months) get a personal "we'd love to see you back" outreach with a **comeback perk** — e.g., a **complimentary re-assessment** or **a discounted return package**. Optionally pair with: "and if you bring the issue that's been bugging a friend, you both get [perk]."
- **Who gets what:** Lapsed patient gets a low-friction reason to return; if they bring a friend, double-sided kicks in.
- **Gate:** Books a return visit within the campaign window.
- **Metric:** **Reactivation** — the cheapest patients to win. ([GymMaster](https://www.gymmaster.com/blog/seasonal-gym-marketing-ideas-to-boost-memberships/))
- **Economics:** Cost = one assessment slot or a discount; upside = re-entered maintenance funnel. Very high ROI because these patients already trust her.
- **A/B test:** Free re-assessment vs. discounted return-pack; measure **% of lapsed list that rebooks within 30 days.** Segment by lapse length (1–3 mo vs. 6+ mo).

### Offer #5 — Family / partner "get better together" package
- **Mechanic:** A patient and a partner/spouse/family member **enroll together** (shared or paired package/membership) at a **bundle rate**, or "refer your household, you both get [perk]."
- **Who gets what:** Both household members get a bundle price or paired perk.
- **Gate:** Both complete enrollment.
- **Metric:** Acquisition + retention — built-in accountability ("we're doing this together") improves adherence and lowers churn (the Hormozi "you'll get better results together" framing). ([learnbizfaster](https://learnbizfaster.com/the-outrageously-easy-way-to-get-new-customers/))
- **Economics:** Slightly lower per-head price for a **bigger basket** and a **stickier pair** (households churn together less). **[NEEDS NUMBERS to set bundle discount.]**
- **A/B test:** Promote household bundle vs. not; measure **avg basket value** and **paired retention at 90 days** vs. solo patients.

### Offer #6 — "Bring a friend" first-visit + Google review *ask* (compliant)
- **Mechanic (two parts):**
  1. **Bring-a-friend first visit:** existing patient can bring a friend to experience a low-commitment first session/consult; the *referrer* earns referral credit only if the friend later **converts** (ties back to Offer #1 gate).
  2. **Review ASK (NOT incentivized):** at the success moment, ask happy patients for an honest Google review with a QR card. **No reward is promised or given for reviews** — thank-you only, after the fact. ([WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/blog/google-review-incentives/), [RaveCapture](https://ravecapture.com/resources/blog/review-incentives-whats-allowed-and-what-actually-works/))
- **Who gets what:** Bring-a-friend = standard referral credit on conversion. Reviews = goodwill only.
- **Gate:** Friend converts (for credit). Reviews = no gate, no reward, ever.
- **Metric:** Acquisition + local search reputation (reviews fuel #1-ranked acquisition channel for a local practice).
- **Compliance:** **Hard rule — never tie any session, discount, or credit to leaving a review.** Ask + thank only.
- **A/B test:** Test review *ask* timing (in-room QR at success moment vs. post-visit text); measure **reviews/month**. Test bring-a-friend signage on vs. off; measure **first-visit referrals.**

- **Referrer retention / membership-renewal at 90 days** (does the program also reduce *their* churn — the retention goal).
