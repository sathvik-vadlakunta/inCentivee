It's the question we hear most often at Hilltop Family Dentistry: *"How often do I actually
need to come in?"* The short answer for most people is **every six months** — but the real
answer depends on you. Here's how to think about it.

## The general rule: twice a year

For most healthy adults and children, a checkup and cleaning **every six months** keeps
plaque and tartar under control and lets us catch small problems before they become
painful, expensive ones. This twice-a-year cadence is the long-standing recommendation of
the American Dental Association.

## Why it matters more than you'd think

Skipping the dentist isn't harmless. According to the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/oral-health/about/),
about **21% of U.S. adults have untreated tooth decay**, and **more than 42% of adults aged
30 and older have some form of gum disease** — much of it preventable. The CDC also reports
that adults who go more than a year between visits have **substantially higher rates of
periodontitis**.

The catch is that early decay and gum disease are usually **painless**. By the time a tooth
hurts, the problem is often advanced. Regular visits are how we catch it while it's small.

## When you might need more frequent visits

Some patients do better with checkups every 3–4 months, including those who:

- Have a history of gum disease
- Are prone to cavities
- Smoke or use tobacco
- Have diabetes or a weakened immune system
- Are pregnant

We'll always recommend a schedule based on **your** mouth, not a one-size-fits-all rule.

## What happens at a checkup

A typical visit at Hilltop includes a professional cleaning, an exam, digital X-rays when
needed, and an oral cancer screening — plus time to answer your questions. It's quick,
comfortable, and the single best thing you can do for your long-term oral health.
