How ChatGPT Decides Which Dentist to Recommend (And How to Be #1)

By Kody Doherty · April 28, 2026 · 9 min read

The AI Search Revolution Is Here

Something fundamental has shifted in how patients find their dentist. Over 40% of patients now ask AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Claude, and Perplexity for dentist recommendations before ever opening Google. This is the single biggest change in patient acquisition since Google Search itself — and most dental practices are completely unprepared for it.

Think about what that means. Nearly half of your potential new patients are typing something like "Who's the best dentist in [your city]?" into ChatGPT — and ChatGPT is answering. It is naming specific practices, describing their specialties, mentioning their reviews, and telling patients whether or not to book with them. The question is: is it naming yours?

For most practices, the answer is no. Not because they are bad dentists, but because they have never optimized for the way AI assistants find and evaluate information. Traditional SEO gets you ranked on Google. AI search optimization — sometimes called GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) or AEO (AI Engine Optimization) — is an entirely different discipline. And right now, the practices that figure it out first will lock in an advantage that compounds for years.

What Happens When Someone Asks ChatGPT "Best Dentist Near Me"

To understand how to win in AI search, you need to understand how these systems actually work. ChatGPT does not have a ranking algorithm like Google. There is no PageRank equivalent, no auction for ad placement. Instead, it does something fundamentally different: it synthesizes information.

When a patient asks ChatGPT for a dentist recommendation, here is what happens behind the scenes:

  1. Training data recall. The model draws on everything it learned during training — dental practice websites it has seen, review sites, directory listings, news articles, and blog posts. If your practice has a strong web presence with lots of high-quality content, you are already embedded in the model's knowledge.
  2. Real-time web search. Most AI assistants now perform live web searches to supplement their training data. ChatGPT uses Bing. Gemini uses Google. Perplexity searches multiple engines. They pull in your Google Business Profile, recent reviews, website content, and directory listings — all in real time.
  3. Retrieval and synthesis. The AI reads through dozens of sources, cross-references the information, and builds a coherent recommendation. It is looking for consistent signals across multiple sources that a practice is reputable, well-reviewed, and relevant to what the patient is asking for.
  4. Response generation. Finally, it writes a natural-language response that typically names 3-5 practices, explains why each one is worth considering, and often includes addresses, phone numbers, and specialties.

The critical insight: AI assistants are not just looking at one source. They are triangulating across everything they can find about your practice. Every review, every directory listing, every page on your website, every mention in a blog post — it all contributes to whether or not the AI trusts you enough to recommend you to a patient.

The 5 Factors AI Assistants Weigh Most Heavily

After analyzing thousands of AI-generated dental recommendations across ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Claude, and Perplexity, we have identified five factors that consistently determine which practices get recommended and which get ignored.

1 Online Presence Strength

AI assistants heavily favor practices with deep, well-maintained websites. This means more than just a homepage and a contact page. The practices that get recommended consistently have dedicated service pages for each procedure (implants, Invisalign, emergency dental, pediatric dentistry), location-specific content that mentions neighborhoods and landmarks, and regularly updated content that signals the practice is active and current.

A five-page website built in 2019 and never updated is essentially invisible to AI. A 40-page website with detailed service descriptions, staff bios, patient education content, and regular blog posts is exactly what AI assistants are looking for when they need to recommend someone.

2 Review Signals

Reviews are perhaps the single most important factor in AI recommendations. AI assistants treat reviews as the strongest form of social proof, and they evaluate them across multiple dimensions: total count (more is better — 200+ Google reviews significantly outperforms 30), average rating (4.7+ is the threshold where AI starts recommending confidently), recency (reviews from the last 90 days matter far more than reviews from 2022), and specificity (reviews that mention specific procedures or staff names carry more weight).

AI assistants also cross-reference reviews across platforms. If you have strong reviews on Google, Healthgrades, Yelp, and Zocdoc, the AI sees a consistent pattern of patient satisfaction. If you only have reviews on one platform, the signal is weaker.

3 Structured Data (Schema Markup)

This is where most practices fail completely — and where the biggest opportunity lies. Schema markup is code on your website that tells AI assistants exactly who you are, what services you offer, where you are located, your hours, your accepted insurance, and more. It is machine-readable information that AI can parse instantly and trust implicitly.

The practices that rank highest in AI recommendations consistently implement LocalBusiness or Dentist schema, FAQPage schema for common patient questions, MedicalProcedure schema for each service they offer, and Review schema that surfaces their star ratings. Without schema markup, AI assistants have to guess what your practice does based on unstructured text. With it, they know exactly what you offer, and they recommend you with confidence.

4 Directory Consistency (NAP)

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number — and consistency across directories is a trust signal that AI assistants take very seriously. When your practice name, address, and phone number are identical across Google Business Profile, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Yelp, the ADA directory, Vitals, WebMD, Yellow Pages, and 15+ other directories, AI sees a trustworthy, established business.

When your information is inconsistent — an old phone number on Yelp, a slightly different practice name on Healthgrades, a suite number missing on Zocdoc — it creates uncertainty. AI assistants will not confidently recommend a practice when they cannot even confirm its correct address. We regularly audit practices and find NAP inconsistencies across 60-70% of their directory listings. Fixing this alone can dramatically improve AI visibility.

5 Authoritative Content

AI assistants are trained to value expertise, and they evaluate your content through that lens. Practices that publish in-depth educational content — blog posts explaining procedures, FAQ pages answering common patient concerns, detailed service pages that demonstrate clinical knowledge — are seen as authoritative sources that AI can cite with confidence.

Content that works especially well for AI visibility includes: procedure explainers ("What to Expect During a Root Canal"), comparison guides ("Dental Implants vs. Bridges: Which Is Right for You?"), local health content ("Fluoride Levels in [City] Water and What It Means for Your Teeth"), and patient education resources. The key is depth and specificity. AI can tell the difference between a 200-word placeholder blog post and a genuinely helpful 1,500-word guide written by someone who knows what they are talking about.

The llms.txt Factor: The New Standard AI Assistants Are Looking For

There is a new standard emerging that most dental practices have never heard of: llms.txt. If you know what robots.txt does — it tells search engine crawlers how to interact with your site — then llms.txt is the equivalent for AI assistants. It is a plain-text file that sits at the root of your website and tells AI exactly what it should know about your practice.

A well-crafted llms.txt file includes your practice name, location, services offered, accepted insurance, notable specialties, staff credentials, and links to your most important pages. When ChatGPT or Gemini visits your website, they check for this file first. It is the fastest, most direct way to control what AI assistants know and say about your practice.

Right now, fewer than 1% of dental practice websites have an llms.txt file. This is an enormous first-mover advantage. The practices that implement it now will be the ones AI assistants learn to trust and recommend consistently.

Why Most Dental Practices Are Invisible to AI

The uncomfortable truth is that the average dental practice website was built for humans browsing Google in 2018 — not for AI assistants synthesizing information in 2026. Here is what we typically find when we audit a practice:

  • No schema markup at all. The website is a collection of unstructured text that AI has to guess at.
  • Fewer than 50 Google reviews, with the most recent one being 4+ months old.
  • Stale content. The last blog post was published in 2022. The website copy has not been updated in years.
  • No llms.txt file. AI assistants have no structured summary to work from.
  • Inconsistent directory listings. Wrong phone numbers, old addresses, missing profiles on key platforms.
  • Generic service pages. One paragraph about "dental implants" instead of a comprehensive guide that demonstrates expertise.

Each of these issues individually hurts your AI visibility. Combined, they make your practice functionally invisible to the 40%+ of patients who are asking AI for help finding a dentist. Those patients are going to your competitors — not because your competitors are better dentists, but because their online presence gives AI the confidence to recommend them.

Real Example: From Invisible to Recommended in 90 Days

One of the most compelling cases we have seen involved a general dentistry practice in Wyoming. When we first ran their PracticeRank audit, they scored 31 out of 100. They had a basic five-page website, 28 Google reviews (4.3 average), no schema markup, no llms.txt, and inconsistent NAP data across directories. When we tested AI recommendations for "best dentist in [their city]," they appeared in 0 out of 10 AI-generated responses.

Over the next 90 days, we implemented a comprehensive AI optimization strategy:

  • Built out 22 new service and location pages with detailed, authoritative content
  • Implemented full schema markup: Dentist, FAQPage, MedicalProcedure, and Review schemas
  • Deployed an llms.txt file with a complete practice summary
  • Launched an automated review request system that generated 85 new Google reviews in 90 days, bringing them to 113 reviews at a 4.8 average
  • Corrected NAP data across 24 directories
  • Published 8 in-depth blog posts targeting high-intent patient questions

At the 90-day mark, we re-tested. Their PracticeRank score had risen to 79 out of 100. They now appeared in 7 out of 10 AI-generated recommendations for dental searches in their area. New patient inquiries increased by 22 per month. The practice owner told us it was the single most impactful marketing investment they had ever made.

What You Can Do Today

If you are a dental practice owner reading this, here are the most impactful steps you can take right now:

  1. Audit your AI visibility. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity "Who is the best dentist in [your city]?" See if your practice appears. If it does not, you know the scope of the problem.
  2. Fix your reviews. If you are below 100 Google reviews or below a 4.7 rating, make review generation your top priority. Implement a post-visit text or email system that asks every patient to leave a review.
  3. Add schema markup. At minimum, add LocalBusiness/Dentist schema to your homepage. If you have the technical capability, add FAQPage and MedicalProcedure schema as well.
  4. Create an llms.txt file. Even a basic version with your practice name, services, location, and key URLs is better than nothing.
  5. Audit your directory listings. Check your NAP consistency across Google, Yelp, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, and the ADA directory. Fix any inconsistencies.
  6. Publish authoritative content. Write detailed service pages and educational blog posts that demonstrate your expertise. Aim for depth over quantity.

These steps will meaningfully improve your AI visibility. But implementing them correctly, maintaining them consistently, and monitoring results across all AI platforms is a significant ongoing effort. That is exactly what PracticeRank was built to do.
