What Is AEO (AI Engine Optimization) for Dentists? The Complete Guide

By Kody Doherty, Founder of PracticeRank · April 28, 2026

A patient in your city just asked ChatGPT: "Who's the best dentist near me for dental implants?"

The AI responded with three recommendations. Your practice was not one of them. Your competitor down the street was listed first.

This scenario is playing out millions of times per day across the country, and it's only accelerating. The practices that show up in these AI-generated recommendations are winning patients before anyone even opens Google. The technology behind getting your practice into those responses is called AI Engine Optimization -- or AEO.

What Is AI Engine Optimization (AEO)?

AI Engine Optimization is the practice of structuring your online presence so that AI assistants -- ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Grok, Claude, Perplexity, Apple Intelligence, and others -- recognize, understand, and recommend your dental practice when patients ask for help.

Think of it this way: SEO is about ranking on a search results page. AEO is about being the answer.

When someone searches Google, they see a list of ten blue links and pick one. When someone asks an AI assistant, they get a direct recommendation -- usually one to three practices, stated with authority. There is no page two. There are no ads to buy your way in. Either the AI recommends you, or it doesn't.

AEO encompasses everything you do to ensure AI systems have the right signals to confidently recommend your practice: structured data on your website, review signals, authoritative content, directory consistency, and emerging standards like llms.txt files that give AI systems a machine-readable summary of who you are and what you do.

Why AEO Matters for Dentists

The shift from search engines to AI assistants is not theoretical -- it is happening right now, and it is happening fast.

Over 40% of patients now use AI assistants to find healthcare providers, including dentists. That number was under 10% two years ago. ChatGPT alone has hundreds of millions of active users. Google's AI Overviews now appear at the top of most dental searches, pushing traditional organic results below the fold. Apple Intelligence is baked into every new iPhone, answering Siri queries with AI-generated summaries.

Here is what makes this different from every other marketing channel:

  • You cannot buy AI visibility. Unlike Google Ads or Facebook Ads, there is no way to pay for placement in an AI response. Visibility must be earned through legitimate signals of quality, authority, and relevance.
  • First movers dominate for years. AI models form associations between locations, specialties, and practices. Once an AI consistently recommends your competitor, it becomes exponentially harder to displace them. The practices optimizing now are building a moat.
  • AI recommendations carry outsized trust. When a patient reads a Google Ad, they know it's paid placement. When ChatGPT or Gemini recommends a specific dentist, patients treat it as an expert, unbiased opinion. Conversion rates from AI recommendations are significantly higher than traditional search.
  • The shift is accelerating, not plateauing. Every major tech company is pouring billions into AI assistants. Google is replacing its own search results with AI Overviews. This is the new default interface for finding services.

The bottom line: if your practice is invisible to AI assistants today, you are losing patients to competitors who are not -- and the gap widens every month.

How AI Assistants Decide Who to Recommend

AI assistants do not have a simple ranking algorithm like Google's PageRank. Instead, they synthesize information from multiple sources to form a judgment about which practices are the best fit for a given query. Understanding what they look at is the key to AEO.

When a patient asks an AI for a dentist recommendation, the system evaluates:

  • Structured data (schema markup) -- JSON-LD markup on your website that explicitly tells AI systems your practice name, address, phone number, services, accepted insurance, hours, and patient reviews in a machine-readable format.
  • Review signals -- Not just your star rating. AI systems analyze review quantity (do you have enough reviews to be statistically significant?), recency (are patients still reviewing you?), sentiment (what specific things do patients praise or criticize?), and diversity (do reviews come from multiple platforms?).
  • Authoritative content -- Detailed, accurate content on your website about the procedures you offer, your credentials, your approach to care. AI systems need substantive text to understand your expertise.
  • Freshness signals -- When was your content last updated? Is your Google Business Profile active? AI systems deprioritize stale, potentially outdated information.
  • llms.txt files -- An emerging web standard where websites provide a plain-text summary specifically designed for AI consumption. Think of it as a resume for your practice that AI assistants can read instantly.
  • Citation consistency -- Your practice name, address, and phone number (NAP) across dozens of directories -- Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Yelp, your state dental board, insurance directories. Inconsistencies create doubt; consistency builds confidence.
  • E-E-A-T signals -- Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Originally a Google concept, these signals are now fundamental to how all AI systems evaluate credibility.

The practices that score well across all of these dimensions are the ones AI systems recommend with confidence. The practices that are weak in multiple areas simply do not appear.

The 7 AEO Signals That Matter

Let's break down each signal in detail, with specific actions you can take.

1. Schema Markup

Schema markup is structured data you add to your website's code (invisible to patients, visible to machines) that explicitly describes your practice. For dentists, four schema types matter most: Dentist (or LocalBusiness), FAQPage, MedicalProcedure, and Review. Properly implemented schema tells AI exactly what services you provide, where you are located, your hours, your ratings, and the procedures you specialize in. Most dental websites have zero schema markup -- adding it is one of the highest-leverage AEO actions you can take.

2. llms.txt File

The llms.txt standard is an emerging convention where websites host a plain-text file at /llms.txt (and optionally a more detailed /llms-full.txt) that provides a structured, machine-readable summary of the site. For a dental practice, this file would include your practice name, location, specialties, accepted insurance, key differentiators, and links to important pages. AI systems are increasingly trained to look for and prioritize this file. Very few dental practices have one today -- implementing it puts you far ahead of the curve.

3. Google Business Profile Completeness and Activity

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is one of the primary data sources AI systems pull from. A complete profile means: every service listed, business hours accurate and up to date, photos uploaded regularly, Q&A answered, posts published weekly, and attributes filled out (wheelchair accessible, accepts new patients, languages spoken). An active, complete GBP signals to AI that your practice is legitimate, operational, and engaged.

4. Review Quantity, Quality, and Recency

AI systems weight reviews heavily because they represent real patient experiences. The formula is straightforward: you need volume (at least 50+ Google reviews to be competitive in most markets), high ratings (4.5+ stars), and recency (new reviews coming in every week, not a burst from two years ago). AI also performs sentiment analysis on review text -- reviews that mention specific procedures, staff names, and positive experiences carry more weight than generic five-star ratings with no text.

5. Content Depth and Freshness

If your website has a one-paragraph blurb about dental implants, AI has almost nothing to work with when a patient asks about implant providers. Contrast that with a practice whose website has a 1,500-word guide covering implant types, candidacy, the procedure timeline, costs, aftercare, and FAQs. AI systems need substantive content to understand your expertise and confidently recommend you for specific procedures. Content also needs to be current -- update key pages at least quarterly.

6. Citation Consistency Across 20+ Directories

Your practice is listed on dozens of directories whether you manage them or not -- Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Vitals, Yelp, Yellow Pages, your state dental association, insurance provider directories, and many more. If your phone number is different on Healthgrades than on Google, or your address is formatted differently on Yelp than on your website, AI systems lose confidence in your data accuracy. Consistent, identical NAP (Name, Address, Phone) data across all directories is a foundational AEO requirement.

7. E-E-A-T Signals

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness -- these are the credibility markers AI systems look for. For dentists, this means: dentist bios with real credentials and education, published case studies or before/after galleries, professional association memberships mentioned on-site, author attribution on blog content, HTTPS security, privacy policies, and clear contact information. AI systems are designed to recommend trustworthy providers, especially in healthcare. Demonstrating E-E-A-T through your web presence makes you a safer recommendation.

AEO vs. SEO: Complementary, Not Competing

A common misconception is that AEO replaces SEO. It does not. The two are complementary, and the best strategy pursues both simultaneously.

SEO gets your practice found when patients search on Google. It focuses on keyword rankings, backlinks, page speed, and technical site health. SEO is still critical -- Google processes billions of searches daily, and organic search traffic remains a major patient acquisition channel.

AEO gets your practice recommended when patients ask AI assistants. It focuses on structured data, review signals, content authority, and machine-readable formats. AEO is becoming critical -- AI-mediated discovery is growing exponentially, and the practices that invest now will dominate these channels for years.

The good news: many AEO best practices also improve your SEO. Adding schema markup helps Google understand your site. Publishing authoritative content ranks better in traditional search and gives AI systems more to work with. Building citation consistency improves local SEO rankings and AI confidence. Review generation helps your Maps ranking and your AI recommendations.

The practices that win in 2026 and beyond are the ones doing both. If your current marketing agency only talks about Google rankings and never mentions ChatGPT, Gemini, or AI search, they are fighting the last war.

Once an AI assistant consistently recommends a specific practice, it creates a reinforcing cycle: more patients, more reviews, stronger signals, even more confident AI recommendations.
