PracticeRank Score v2

Rebuild the 0–100 score so it reliably shows real progress within 4–6 weeks, is robust to missing/flaky data, and is driven by the work we actually do — not by lagging signals we don't control.

Owner: Kody · Status: Spec (draft) · 2026-06-17 · specs/active/practicerank-score-v2.html · supersedes geo_agent/practicerank_score.py v1

1. Why v1 can't show 4–6 week progress

v1 pillarweightproblem for near-term progress
AI Visibility30%✓ good — moves in weeks, we influence it
Search Growth25%Organic rankings/traffic take months, and most customers have no GSC connected (Paradigm = 0 data). 25% on a lagging, often-missing signal.
Technical Health15%Built on PageSpeed seo_score/perf_score → when PageSpeed times out it's 0, tanking the pillar (Paradigm Tech = 15). API flakiness ≠ regression.
Content Velocity15%OK but underweighted — this is work we 100% control.
Reputation15%Review volume grows slowly; 15% on a months-scale signal.

Net effect: Paradigm's score dropped −3 the same week we shipped 32 content pieces, 13 location pages, schema, FAQ, and llms.txt — because the things we shipped barely move the number, while a PageSpeed timeout and missing GSC data dragged it down. The score punishes us for data gaps and rewards nothing for the playbook.

2. Design principles

  • Lead with what we control and what moves in weeks. The GEO foundation (schema, llms.txt, robots, FAQ, location pages) and content velocity go from ~0 → ~100% in the first 4–6 weeks as we run the playbook. That should be the engine of early progress.
  • De-weight lagging/external signals. Organic rankings/traffic (months) and review volume (slow) stay in the score as outcome confirmation, but at low weight — and never tank it when absent.
  • Missing data is neutral, not zero. No connected GSC / failed PageSpeed → use a neutral baseline or carry-forward last good value. A timeout must not look like a regression.
  • Smooth + monotonic-friendly. Completion pillars only ratchet up as we ship; rolling averages absorb noise (AI non-determinism, API flakiness).
  • Baseline + trajectory. Lock a week-0 baseline; always show "+N since baseline" and an expected 6-week trajectory, so progress is the headline, not the absolute number.

Re-weighted so the two things we control and ship fast (Foundation + Content) drive ~50%, AI 30%, and the laggy/external signals drop to 20% combined.

5. Baseline & the 4–6 week progress model

Make progress the headline, designed so the standard playbook visibly moves the number.

  • Lock a baseline at onboarding (week 0) — the score before we touch anything. Store as baseline_score + date on the customer.
  • Every report shows "{score} · +{score − baseline} since {month}" and a 6-week sparkline.
  • Expected trajectory from running the playbook (illustrative, Foundation+Content do the early lifting):

1–2schema, llms.txt, robots, FAQ schema live753520~50

Concrete promise to the client: "measurable progress within 4–6 weeks." Foundation + Content are mostly us executing, so they reliably climb regardless of GSC/competitor externals — that's what makes the early curve dependable.
