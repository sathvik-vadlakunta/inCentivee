# AI Search Is Replacing Google for Patients — Is Your Practice Invisible?

Something fundamental shifted in how people find a dentist.

Instead of typing "best dentist near me" into Google, a growing number of patients are asking AI assistants directly: *"Who's the best dentist in [city] for dental implants?"*

And the AI answers. With specific names. Specific practices. Specific reasons why.

If your practice isn't one of those names, you have a problem that traditional SEO alone cannot fix.

## The Shift Is Already Happening

ChatGPT now has over [900 million weekly active users](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users/) and processes roughly 2.5 billion queries per day. Google's own AI Overviews now appear on [up to 60% of U.S. search queries](https://xponent21.com/insights/google-ai-overviews-surpass-60-percent/), according to Advanced Web Ranking data from April 2026. Perplexity handles an estimated [30+ million queries daily](https://www.demandsage.com/perplexity-ai-statistics/). And Apple is building AI-powered search directly into Siri, [powered by Google's Gemini](https://searchengineland.com/apple-world-knowledge-answers-ai-search-461569), launching later this year.

When a patient asks one of these AI engines for a dentist recommendation, the AI doesn't show 10 blue links. It gives **one to three specific answers** — practices it considers the most relevant, credible, and well-documented.

This is fundamentally different from traditional search. In Google, you compete for position on a page of results. In AI search, you either get mentioned or you don't exist.

## Why Most Practices Are Invisible to AI

We've analyzed AI search visibility across hundreds of practices. The pattern is consistent: **most are completely invisible to AI search engines.**

The reasons are straightforward:

**AI engines don't crawl the web like Google does.** They were trained on data, and they pull real-time information from structured sources. If your practice's information isn't in the formats AI engines understand, they simply don't know about you.

**Your Google ranking doesn't transfer.** A practice ranking #1 on Google for "dentist in Austin" can still be completely absent from ChatGPT's recommendations for the same query. These are different systems with different signals.

**Reviews and reputation matter differently.** AI engines weigh credentialed expertise, structured data, and authoritative content more heavily than backlink profiles. The signals that got you to page 1 on Google are not the same signals that get you recommended by AI.

## What Patients Actually See

When someone asks ChatGPT "Who's the best cosmetic dentist in Salt Lake City?", the response typically includes:

- 3-5 specific practice names
- Why each practice is recommended (specialties, credentials, patient reviews)
- Location and contact information
- A brief comparison between options

The practices that appear in these responses get the equivalent of a **personal referral from a trusted source** — at scale, 24/7, to every patient who asks.

The practices that don't appear get nothing. No impression. No click. No awareness that they even exist.

## The Window Is Closing

Right now, AI search optimization is still early. Most practices haven't even thought about it. That creates a massive first-mover advantage.

The practices that optimize for AI visibility now will build a compounding lead. AI engines develop preferences based on the information available to them. The sooner your practice is well-represented in AI-readable formats, the more entrenched your position becomes.

Waiting 12-18 months means competing against practices that have already established themselves as the AI-recommended option in your market.

## What This Means for Your Practice

This isn't about abandoning Google SEO — that still matters and will for years. This is about recognizing that a **second front has opened** in patient acquisition, and most practices are completely undefended.

The practices that will thrive in 2026 and beyond are the ones that show up in both traditional search and AI search. The ones that appear when patients ask Google *and* when they ask ChatGPT.

The question isn't whether AI search will affect your patient volume. It's whether you'll be on the winning or losing side of that shift.
