ChatGPT Is Recommending Your Competitor. Here's What That's Costing You.

Try this right now. Open ChatGPT and type:

"Who's the best dentist in [your city] for [your top service]?"

Read the response carefully. Look at which practices get named. Look at the reasons given. Look at the level of detail and confidence in each recommendation.

Now ask yourself: is your practice in that answer?

If it's not — and statistically, it probably isn't — every patient who asks that same question is being directed to your competitors. Not to a search results page where you might show up. Directly to your competitors, by name, with reasons to choose them.

The Invisible Referral Machine

Think about how powerful a personal referral is. When a trusted friend says "Go see Dr. Smith, she's the best cosmetic dentist in town" — that patient almost always books with Dr. Smith.

AI search is a referral engine at scale. When ChatGPT tells a patient "Dr. Smith at Bright Smile Dental is highly regarded for cosmetic dentistry in Portland," that carries similar weight to a personal recommendation. The patient didn't see a list of 10 options. They were given a curated, confident answer.

And unlike a human referral, AI search operates 24/7, across thousands of queries per day, in your market.

What This Actually Costs

Let's put conservative numbers on it.

If just 20 potential patients per month in your area ask AI for dentist recommendations — and your competitor appears while you don't — you're losing 20 potential first-visit patients per month.

At an average lifetime patient value of $4,500-$22,000 for a dental practice (commonly cited at roughly $10,000 industry-wide), that's $90,000 to $200,000 in lifetime revenue lost every single month.

You'll never see these patients in your analytics. They'll never visit your website. They'll never show up as a missed call. They simply went to the practice AI recommended and never considered you.

This is the most dangerous kind of competitive loss — the kind you can't see.

Why Your Competitor Shows Up and You Don't

AI engines don't randomly pick favorites. When ChatGPT recommends a practice, it's because that practice has — intentionally or accidentally — provided the signals AI needs to feel confident in recommending them.

Common reasons a competitor appears and you don't:

They have more structured, verifiable information online. Their business entity is well-documented across multiple authoritative sources. AI can confirm who they are, what they do, and where they're located.

Their content demonstrates genuine expertise. They have in-depth content about their specialties that reads like it was written by (or for) actual dental professionals. AI engines are trained to distinguish expert content from generic marketing copy.

Their reputation is broader and more consistent. It's not just Google reviews. Their credentials, affiliations, patient testimonials, and professional presence create a complete picture that AI can reference with confidence.

They may be doing this intentionally. An increasing number of forward-thinking practices and marketing agencies are actively optimizing for AI search. If your competitor is among them, the gap will only widen.

The Five-Engine Problem

It's not just ChatGPT. There are now five major AI search engines that patients use:

  1. ChatGPT — The largest, with 900M+ weekly active users
  2. Google Gemini / AI Overviews — Integrated directly into Google Search, appearing on up to 60% of queries
  3. Perplexity — 100M+ monthly active users, growing fastest among research-oriented users
  4. Claude — Roughly 30M monthly active users, popular for detailed, nuanced recommendations
  5. Grok — 117M monthly active users, integrated into X's 550M-user ecosystem

Each engine has its own signals and biases. A practice might appear in Perplexity but not ChatGPT. Or rank well in Claude but be invisible on Gemini.

This fragmentation means you can't optimize for one engine and assume coverage across all of them. A comprehensive AI visibility strategy must address all five.

The Competitive Intelligence Gap

Here's what makes this even more challenging: you can't see what's happening.

With Google SEO, you can track your rankings, monitor your competitors, and measure your progress with standard tools. The data is transparent.

With AI search, there's no equivalent of "checking your ranking." AI responses are dynamic, contextual, and vary based on how the question is asked. Without systematic monitoring across all engines, all relevant queries, and all your key service areas, you're flying blind.

Most practices don't even know they're losing patients to AI recommendations. By the time they notice a dip in new patient volume, the competitor's advantage is already entrenched.

What You Can Do About It

The good news: AI visibility is still early enough that first-movers win disproportionately. Most practices in most markets haven't done anything about AI search optimization. The bar to becoming AI's top recommendation is lower today than it will be in a year.

But you need three things:

  1. Visibility into where you stand. Across all five AI engines, for the queries patients actually ask, compared to your competitors. Without this data, you're guessing.

  2. A strategy that addresses AI's specific signals. Not just more SEO. AI engines have different priorities than Google's traditional algorithm. The technical and content work required is related but distinct.

  3. Ongoing monitoring. AI recommendations change as engines update their models and as competitors improve their visibility. This isn't a one-time fix — it requires continuous tracking and optimization.

The practices that act now will own the AI recommendations in their market for years. The ones that wait will spend significantly more to catch up — if they can catch up at all.
